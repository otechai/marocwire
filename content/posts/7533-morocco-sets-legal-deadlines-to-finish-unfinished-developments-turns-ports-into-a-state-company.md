# Morocco Sets Hard Deadlines to Finish Unfinished Land Developments, Turns Its Ports Into One State Company

*Sourced from Morocco's Official Gazette (Bulletin Officiel), Issue No. 7533, published August 10, 2026*

## 🏗️ If You Bought Land in Morocco, This Law Is For You

Morocco has rewritten the rules governing real-estate subdivisions — the "lotissements" where developers buy raw land, promise roads/water/electricity, and sell plots before finishing the work. This is the process behind countless stalled housing projects that diaspora buyers have complained about for years. Developers now face hard, size-based deadlines to complete infrastructure: 3 years for subdivisions up to 20 hectares, 5 years up to 100 hectares, 7 years up to 250 hectares, 10 years up to 400 hectares, and 15 years for anything larger. Miss the deadline and the building authorization simply expires. Crucially, the state and local authorities now have explicit power to step in, finish abandoned projects themselves, and bill the developer for it — and a new program will regularize existing informal ("illegal") subdivisions, while banning new subdivisions in flood-, earthquake-, or water-protection-risk zones. Once infrastructure is provisionally accepted, roads and utility land automatically and freely transfer to the local municipality, which should speed up title registration for buyers.
*— Law 34.21 amending Law 25.90, enacted by royal decree 1.26.61, July 28, 2026*

## ⚓ Morocco's Ports Become One Company

The state-run National Ports Agency has been converted into a single joint-stock company, wholly owned by the government, called "Moroccan Ports" (Moanuu Al Maghrib). It will manage, upgrade, and maintain essentially all of Morocco's ports (the Tangier Med free-zone port and the Marchica lagoon development keep their own separate regimes), set port tariffs and dues, oversee terminal operators and concession holders, and can even create subsidiaries or take on infrastructure project-management contracts for other government bodies. It's part of a broader corporatization push tied to Morocco's port-and-logistics buildout ahead of the 2030 World Cup and its Atlantic-corridor ambitions (Nador West Med, Dakhla Atlantic).
*— Law 34.25, royal decree 1.26.56, July 28, 2026*

## 🏦 New Rules to Stop a Bank Collapse — and Protect Your Deposits

Morocco has adopted a modern bank "resolution" regime, similar to post-2008 reforms in the US and EU. Bank Al-Maghrib (the central bank) can now intervene early in a struggling bank — demanding recovery plans, replacing management, or, as a last resort, forcing a sale of the business, spinning off a "bridge bank," or splitting off bad assets — instead of waiting for a bank to fail outright. The existing deposit-guarantee fund, funded by mandatory bank contributions, can now also be used to help finance a bank's rescue, not just reimburse depositors after a collapse. The strictest rules apply to banks judged "systemically important" — i.e., too big to fail.
*— Law 87.21 amending Law 103.12 and the Bank Al-Maghrib charter (Law 40.17), royal decree 1.26.65, July 28, 2026*

## 💰 The 2024 Books: Morocco Closes the Year With a ~36.8 Billion Dirham Surplus

Morocco's official 2024 budget settlement law shows the state took in more than it spent, ending the year roughly 36.8 billion dirhams (about $3.7 billion) ahead across its general budget, special funds, and autonomous agencies combined. A few figures worth knowing: regions and municipalities received 45.7 billion dirhams from VAT revenue-sharing (more than budgeted); the fund managing the aftermath of Morocco's 2023 earthquake disbursed over 8.6 billion dirhams in 2024; the social protection and cohesion fund paid out nearly 24.7 billion dirhams; and the state's housing-and-urban-integration solidarity fund spent 5.6 billion dirhams — well above its original 2 billion dirham budget, suggesting housing demand outpaced planning.
*— Settlement Law 04.26, royal decree 1.26.57, July 28, 2026*

## 🗺️ Urban Planning Agencies Get a Regional Overhaul

Morocco is replacing its old system of "Agences Urbaines" (Urban Agencies) with new Regional Agencies for Urban Planning and Housing, one per region, matching the country's 12-region map. These bodies review zoning plans, sign off on building and subdivision permits, and police illegal construction — the goal is more consistent planning that spans both cities and the rural areas around them, which matters for anyone building or buying property outside major city centers.
*— Law 64.23, royal decree 1.26.63, July 28, 2026*

## 🐾 A New National Law to Protect Stray Animals

Morocco has passed its first dedicated law on stray dog and cat welfare, a topic that's drawn international attention and criticism over the years (including around past World Cup bids). It sets up municipal and private shelters, mandates capture-neuter-vaccinate-release programs, creates a national digital registry, and criminalizes cruelty — killing or torturing a stray can now mean up to six months in prison and fines. Existing shelters and operators get up to two years to come into compliance.
*— Law 19.25, royal decree 1.26.55, July 28, 2026*

## ✍️ Morocco's Notaries (Adoul) Go Digital

The profession of "Adoul" — the Islamic-law notaries who authenticate marriage, inheritance, and property contracts — has been overhauled with mandatory electronic recordkeeping, digital signatures, tougher entry requirements, and new oversight councils. This matters for diaspora Moroccans handling inheritance or property paperwork remotely, since digitized notarial records should make it easier to obtain and verify documents from abroad.
*— Law 051.26, royal decree 1.26.58, July 28, 2026*

### Also in this issue
- Regions' organic law amended: regions gain new powers over economic zones and industrial land, and the state must transfer at least 12 billion dirhams/year to regions starting in fiscal year 2027.
- A new National Statistical Council and reorganized High Commission for Planning were created to oversee official statistics.
- Morocco's National School of Administration was renamed and restructured; entry exams treated as civil-service recruitment.
- The National Council of Physicians/Doctors law was overhauled (elections, disciplinary rules, continuing-education duties).

- The National Road Safety Agency's staff-secondment arrangements were extended.
- Copyright law (Law 2.00) was amended to strengthen collection of royalties for public-domain-adjacent works and enforcement tools against piracy.
- The professional journalists' statute was updated, extending protections to reporters at broadcasters and news agencies, not just print outlets.
- Tax exemptions for companies that train job-seekers ("insertion" contracts) were extended and clarified.
