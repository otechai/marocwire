## 🏛️ Six lawmakers resign in ten days — Constitutional Court moves fast to fill the seats

Morocco's Constitutional Court issued five separate rulings on the same day, Thursday, July 16, 2026, formally declaring six parliamentary seats vacant after a wave of resignations hit both chambers within a span of just ten days. Khalid El Ajli and Noureddine Kachibal quit the House of Representatives (their resignation letters landed with the chamber's president on July 6 and July 13 respectively), while Aziz Meknif stepped down from the House of Councillors, representing the Oriental region's local and regional councils electoral college. Ahmed Dahi resigned on July 14, and Abdelmajid Fassi Fihri and Khalid Hatimi both quit on July 15 — the last three resignations coming so close to the end of the legislative session that the chamber's president had to forward them to the Constitutional Court under an eight-day emergency procedure.

For the House of Representatives seats, the Court simply declared the seats vacant. For the House of Councillors seat, the ruling goes further: it orders that the next candidate on the same electoral list take over the seat for the remainder of the current term, per Morocco's organic law on the Councillors' chamber. No public reason was given in the rulings for any of the six resignations. *Source: Constitutional Court rulings 268.26 through 272.26, issued July 16, 2026 (1 Safar 1448).*

## 🩺 More foreign medical degrees now recognized in Morocco — good news for diaspora doctors

Morocco's Ministry of Higher Education and Scientific Research quietly expanded, through a batch of decrees issued in June 2026, the official list of foreign medical, pharmacy and biology diplomas that can be converted into Moroccan specialist qualifications. New entries cover gastroenterology and neurology degrees from Romania and Ukraine, general medicine and general practitioner qualifications from Ukraine, Russia and Libya, a general-medicine doctorate from Madagascar, a radiology specialization from Tunisia, and several clinical-biology/lab-diagnostics qualifications from Ukraine and Russia. In every case, the foreign degree only counts once the holder also passes an evaluation exam validated by a Moroccan medical or pharmacy faculty and the sector's national health committee — and in most cases after a supervised training period of one to three years. This matters directly for Moroccan-origin doctors who trained in Eastern Europe, Russia, North Africa or sub-Saharan Africa and want a legal path to practice back home. *Source: Ministry of Higher Education decrees 1204.26–1213.26 and 1247.26–1253.26, dated June 11–12, 2026.*

## 🦐 Three new marine farms licensed — shrimp, oysters and premium fish join Morocco's aquaculture push

Morocco handed out three new marine aquaculture concessions in June 2026, each running for 10 years and jointly signed by the Deputy Minister for Budget and the Secretary of State for Sea Fishing. Bio Agro Aquicole du Sud, registered in Laâyoune, gets 7.75 hectares off Tarfaya province to farm whiteleg shrimp (*Penaeus vannamei*). Transis Afrique Traders, registered in Dakhla, gets roughly 5.3 hectares in the Oualidia lagoon (Sidi Bennour province) to farm Pacific oysters and grooved carpet-shell clams. And Searulea, registered in Casablanca, gets a much larger 375-hectare concession off Ras El Ma near Nador to farm gilthead seabream, seabass, meagre and greater amberjack — paying a fixed annual royalty of 187,935 dirhams plus a variable fee of 0.1% of everything it sells. Separately, a Casablanca-based inspection firm, Certibio, was accredited for three years to certify Moroccan farm and aquaculture products as genuinely organic. *Source: joint decrees 1286.26, 1287.26, 1296.26 and decree 1307.26, dated June 16–24, 2026.*

## 🚌 Marrakech makes it official: all intercity buses must use the Azouzia station

Morocco's Transport Ministry issued a decree formally requiring every public passenger transport operator whose routes start, end, or simply pass through Marrakech to use the city's official Azouzia road-transport station for picking up or dropping off passengers, luggage and freight. Boarding, alighting, and ticket sales anywhere outside that station are now explicitly banned, replacing a looser 1981-era rule. The move is aimed at cleaning up informal pickup points scattered around the city. *Source: Transport Ministry decree 1302.26, dated June 23, 2026.*

## 🌊 Mehiris port gets its layout plan approved

In Morocco's far south, the Equipment and Water Ministry approved the internal layout ("aménagement") plan for Mehiris port in Aousserd province (Dakhla-Oued Ed-Dahab region), following review by the National Ports Agency's board and consultation with the land-use and housing ministry. The approval clears the way for the port's physical development to proceed according to the validated design. *Source: decree 2524.25, dated October 24, 2025 (published in this issue).*
