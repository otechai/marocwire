## 🌊 Lead: OCP to spin off two new companies to desalinate and pipe seawater to central and northern Morocco

Morocco's state phosphate giant OCP is being authorized to create two dedicated subsidiaries — under the brand "OCP Green Water" — to desalinate seawater and pipe it inland, in a bid to secure the country's industrial water supply and boost water sovereignty. One subsidiary will serve the central region (Safi, Benguerir, Youssoufia, Marrakech), the other the northern region (Jorf Lasfar, El Jadida, Casablanca, Khouribga). Combined, the two are meant to deliver roughly **195 million cubic meters of water a year for the central zone and 215 million cubic meters a year for the northern zone** — about 410 million cubic meters annually — for industrial use, drinking water, and irrigation. The plan is explicitly tied to a state contract requiring OCP to help secure Morocco's water sovereignty, and is meant to also unlock structured financing and public-private partnerships around the new desalination assets. The authorization is valid for one year from publication, during which the subsidiaries must actually be formed.

*Source: Decree No. 2.26.547, dated July 23, 2026 (8 Safar 1448).*

## 🚗 Morocco overhauls its car and motorcycle registration system, with electronic titles and a 2027 deadline for mopeds

Morocco's Transport Ministry has rewritten large parts of the rulebook governing how vehicles — cars, trailers, farm equipment, motorcycles, and now mopeds/light quads — get registered and titled, with a major push toward electronic registration certificates and digital ID checks. Notably, starting **January 1, 2027**, mopeds and light three- and four-wheeled vehicles will be brought into the same registration regime as cars, with new plate formats and design specs (detailed down to plate dimensions and font sizes) attached to the decree. Buyers of new mopeds acquired in Morocco get a grace period through **December 31, 2027** before needing certain proof-of-sale paperwork. Older-style plates can keep being used for a transition period — until the first change of ownership for regular-series plates, and through **December 31, 2026** for vehicles used in international travel. This is a bureaucratic-sounding change, but it affects every car owner and buyer in the country and streamlines a process diaspora Moroccans dealing with vehicles back home will run into.

*Source: Ministerial order No. 640.26, dated March 26, 2026 (7 Shawwal 1447).*

## 🦪 Three new marine fish/shellfish farms authorized off Morocco's Atlantic coast

The government greenlit three new aquaculture concessions along the coast, each tied to a formal 10-year (renewable) agreement with the state. **Marine Mussels Sarl** gets a 15-hectare seaweed farm off Essaouira province growing red, green and brown algae species. **Tri-Marine Sarl**, already operating one farm, received a published excerpt of an amendment extending its concession, also off Essaouira. **Agdoul Mar Sarl AU** gets an 8-hectare clam farm ("Ruditapes decussatus," a local clam known as "lemima") in Dakhla Bay in the far south. Each concession carries a small fixed annual fee (7,500 dirhams for the mussel farm, 80 dirhams for the clam farm) plus a variable royalty of 0.1% of sales — modest by design, since the real value is the state formally opening these coastal waters to aquaculture investment.

*Source: Joint orders No. 1283.26, 1284.26 and 1285.26, dated June 16, 2026 (30 Dhu al-Hijjah 1447).*

## 🏦 CFG Bank licensed as a clearing member on Morocco's derivatives market

Morocco's Ministry of Economy and Finance has approved CFG Bank as an authorized member responsible for trading and clearing on the country's futures/derivatives market for financial instruments — a incremental but concrete sign of Morocco's financial market infrastructure maturing and opening to more players.

*Source: Order No. 1423.26, dated July 8, 2026 (22 Muharram 1448).*

## 💰 National Savings Fund reward rate adjusted, with a temporary boost for postal bank savers

Morocco's Ministry of Economy and Finance tweaked the formula used to calculate returns paid to Barid Bank (the postal savings bank) on deposits held via the National Savings Fund (Caisse d'Épargne Nationale), which is managed by the state deposit-and-management fund CDG. The annual reward rate is now pegged partly to a fixed 1.15% base, with an added bonus running only through **December 31, 2026** — relevant to ordinary Moroccan savers who use postal savings accounts, a common product for lower-income households and rural communities.

*Source: Order No. 982.26, dated May 18, 2026 (1 Dhu al-Hijjah 1447).*

## 🏆 Morocco tweaks the rules for its national "Morocco Youth Prize"

The government reorganized how the "Jaïza al-Maghrib lil-Chabab" (Morocco Prize for Youth) is judged: instead of one process, the ministry responsible for youth affairs will now appoint five separate juries, one per prize category, with clearer rules on eligibility (candidates can't sit on a jury judging their own category, and works that already won another national or international prize are excluded) and clearer procedures for splitting or withholding an award.

*Source: Decree No. 2.24.986, dated July 23, 2026 (8 Safar 1448), amending Decree No. 2.23.361.*

## 🌾 Nine agricultural input companies newly certified to sell certified seeds and rootstock

Morocco's Agriculture Ministry certified nine companies (including Issil Pepinière, Pepinière Al Karam, Agrisafran, Semillas Fito Maroc, Progaines, Golden Plant, Domaine Infinity Plant, Genotic Seeds, and Phoenix Agrotech) to market certified seeds and rootstock — covering olives, grapevines, figs, prickly pears, pomegranates, argan, pome fruit, stone fruit, citrus, saffron corms, corn, legumes, oilseeds, vegetable seeds, and date palm rootstock. Each certification runs five years and is renewable. This is routine but relevant to Morocco's broader push to formalize and professionalize its seed supply chain amid climate and water pressures on agriculture.

*Source: Various orders, Nos. 1308.26–1316.26 and 1322.26, dated June 23–24, 2026.*

## 🏘️ Rural development plan approved for Bni Salah, Chefchaouen province

The governor of Tangier-Tétouan-Al Hoceïma region approved a rural urban-development master plan for the center of Bni Salah commune in Chefchaouen province, following public consultation held over August 2025 — a small but concrete example of Morocco's ongoing rural planning push in the north.

*Source: Order No. 1145.26, dated June 1, 2026 (15 Dhu al-Hijjah 1447).*
