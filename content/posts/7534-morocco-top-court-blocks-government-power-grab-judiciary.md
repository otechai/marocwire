## ⚖️ Constitutional Court: the government can't appoint the judiciary's own senior staff

Morocco's Constitutional Court has struck down part of a law that would have let the government appoint, by decree in the Council of Government, senior officials described in the text as "general judicial curators" — officials who work exclusively inside Morocco's Supreme Judicial Council, the constitutionally independent body that runs the country's court system. The Court ruled that letting the executive branch control who fills that post would compromise the judiciary's constitutionally guaranteed administrative and financial independence, since appointments to any responsibility inside the Supreme Judicial Council must come from the judicial branch itself, not the government.

This matters beyond legal circles: it's a live test of the separation of powers in Morocco, with the country's top constitutional body siding with judicial independence over an attempt by the executive to extend its reach into how the courts manage their own people. The Court let the rest of the underlying law proceed, ordering only that the disputed post be struck from the list of government-appointed senior positions before the law is enacted — which is exactly what happened in a subsequent royal decree.

In a related ruling issued the same week, the Court said it was unable to rule on a separate constitutional challenge to Morocco's new law regulating the legal profession, because whoever referred the case failed to attach a certified copy of the law's actual text — a procedural gap that left the challenge dead on arrival, allowing the law to move forward unexamined on the merits.

*Source: Constitutional Court decisions 278.26 and 277.26, both issued 26 Safar 1448 (10 August 2026).*

## 🤖 Morocco launches four new state institutes for AI and digital transformation

The government has formally created four new public-interest groupings — all branded "Institut Jazari" — to anchor Morocco's push into artificial intelligence and digital transformation under the "Digital Morocco 2030" strategy. Each is set up for 30 years and brings together a long list of ministries (finance, education, higher education, infrastructure, youth, digital transition, and more) alongside the Digital Development Agency, the Rabat-Salé-Kénitra regional council, and Morocco's main business federation (CGEM).

- **Institut Jazari Nawat**, headquartered in Rabat, will focus on applied AI research, sovereign AI infrastructure, data centers, and high-performance computing, and on training public and private talent for the state's digital transformation.
- **Institut Jazari for Education and Technological Innovation**, focused on building tech talent and innovation in education.
- **Institut Jazari Industry X.O**, based at a university, aimed at advanced/next-generation industry.
- **Institut Jazari Smart City**, focused on smart-city solutions.

Together they're presented as a push to modernize Morocco's public administration, boost regional cohesion ("advanced regionalization"), improve digital public services, and position Morocco as a regional innovation hub. This is a real bet on tech investment and skills — worth watching for MREs in tech and business looking at opportunities back home.

*Source: Joint ministerial decisions 1535.26, 1618.26, 1619.26, and 1620.26, dated between 1 and 7 Safar 1448 (16–22 July 2026).*

## 🏘️ Sidi Slimane province gets a new urban development plan

The government approved a new urban development plan and zoning regulation covering Sidi Yahya El Gharb commune and parts of the Amer Chamalia and Amer Sfliya communes in Sidi Slimane province, and formally declared it a matter of public interest — a step that typically precedes infrastructure and construction permitting in the area, following public consultations held in mid-2025.

*Source: Decree 2.26.596, dated 21 Safar 1448 (5 August 2026).*
