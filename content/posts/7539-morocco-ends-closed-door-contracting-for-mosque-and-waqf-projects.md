## 🕌 Morocco's mosque and endowment money can no longer be spent behind closed doors

For the first time, Morocco has written detailed public-procurement rules for the **"Awqaf"** — the vast network of religious endowments (mosque properties, charitable land, and religious-institution assets) managed by the Ministry of Islamic Affairs. Until now, how this administration awarded construction, supply, and service contracts wasn't governed by the same transparency rules that apply to the rest of the Moroccan state. That changes under a new decree, which explicitly grounds every Awqaf contract in principles of free access to competition, equal treatment of bidders, transparency, **integrity, and "good governance."**

The practical changes are significant. Contracts worth less than **1,000,000 dirhams** (roughly $100,000) can go through a simplified tender, but anything above **2,000,000 dirhams** requires a full open call for bids. Direct, negotiated (no-bid) contracts are now capped at just **300,000 dirhams**. Every tender notice, bidding document, and results notice must be published not just in print, but on Morocco's national public-procurement portal and the Ministry's own website — including for contracts to restore historic and traditional mosques and endowment buildings. Bidders can also file formal complaints about how a tender was run, and a share of contracts must be set aside for small and very small businesses, cooperatives, and self-employed contractors.

Why this matters beyond bureaucracy: Awqaf assets touch ordinary religious life directly — mosque construction and renovation, Quranic school facilities, carpets and sound systems for mosques, and restoration of historic Islamic monuments — projects diaspora Moroccans often donate toward or take pride in. Moving that spending onto a public, competitive, auditable footing is a concrete accountability upgrade, even though it arrives as a technical procurement decree rather than a headline reform.

*Source: Decree (Order) of the Minister of Islamic Affairs No. 348.26, dated February 13, 2026 (24 Sha'ban 1447).*
