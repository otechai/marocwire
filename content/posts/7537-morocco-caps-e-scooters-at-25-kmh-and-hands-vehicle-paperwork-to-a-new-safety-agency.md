## 🛴 Morocco rewrites the rules of the road: e-scooters capped, registration goes digital

The government has overhauled Morocco's road-safety and vehicle-registration rules in two linked decrees, one of which was actually signed back in October 2025 but is only now being published.

The headline changes:

- **E-scooters and other "personal mobility devices"** are now formally defined in law and capped at **25 km/h** in built-up areas. Riders of e-scooters, pedal-assist bikes, mopeds, and motorcycles must now wear an approved, strapped-on helmet — a requirement previously limited mainly to motorcyclists. Outside city limits, these devices may only be ridden where a dedicated cycle lane exists.
- **Hands-free/headset bans while driving** are extended explicitly to cover cyclists and riders of motorized two-, three- and four-wheeled vehicles, not just car drivers.
- **Breakdowns**: if your vehicle fails or loses cargo on the road, you must now trigger your hazard lights and place a warning triangle at least 50 meters behind the vehicle, especially near intersections, bends, tunnels and rail crossings.
- **Who's exempt from some rules**: police, Royal Gendarmerie, armed forces, civil protection and ambulance drivers on duty, plus — notably — Bank Al-Maghrib's cash-and-valuables transport vehicles when under armed escort.
- **Registration authority moves**: vehicle type-approval and registration, previously handled through the Ministry of Equipment and Transport, is being transferred to the **National Road Safety Agency (ANSR)**, which will also take over the diplomatic, temporary-import, and administrative vehicle registration series. Used vehicles imported under the temporary or diplomatic series generally can't be registered there if they're older than five years.

Most provisions took effect on publication; the specific rules for e-scooters and pedal-assist bikes phase in six months later, giving importers and riders a grace period.

*Source: Decree No. 2.24.393 of October 3, 2025, and Decree No. 2.25.145 of July 31, 2026, both amending Decree No. 2.10.421/2.10.420 of September 29, 2010 implementing the Road Traffic Code (Law 52.05).*

## 💧 A new dam and two drinking-water pipelines get the green light

Three separate public-utility declarations clear the way for water infrastructure in different corners of the country — the kind of on-the-ground project that matters most to families with land or relatives back home.

- **Azilal**: The government has declared it in the public interest to build a **dam on Oued Lakhdar** near the Aït Majdane commune, and has begun expropriating the private land parcels needed for the works.
- **Tarfaya**: A new **water pipeline will carry drinking water to the Akhfennir commune from a seawater desalination plant**, with the state expropriating a plot of land to lay it. The right to expropriate has been delegated to the head of ONEE, Morocco's national water and electricity utility.
- **El Jadida**: A separate declaration clears land — spread across eight technical sub-plans covering the Ouled Hssain commune — to **supply the province of El Jadida with drinking water**, following a public inquiry held from March to May 2024.

Together the three decrees signal continued state investment in drinking-water supply and dam capacity in drought-exposed regions, though the gazette text doesn't specify budgets, capacity in cubic meters, or completion dates for any of the three.

*Source: Decrees No. 2.26.546, 2.26.550 and 2.26.561, all dated August 3, 2026.*

## ⚓ Safi's new port zone gets another extension

The government has, for at least the third time since 2013, **renewed the public-utility declaration** covering the land needed to develop the port zone next to Safi's new port project, in the Marrakech-Safi region. Renewals like this typically keep expropriation rights alive for the state without adding new land or new commitments — the gazette text doesn't give a reason for the repeated renewal or a new completion date, so it's unclear whether the underlying project has been delayed.

*Source: Decree No. 2.26.563 of August 3, 2026.*

## 🛣️ Tetouan–Amsa road widening also renewed

A separate decree renews the public-utility declaration for **widening (doubling) National Road N16 between Tetouan and Amsa**, covering the Azla and Zaouiat Sidi Kacem communes in Tetouan province. As with the Safi port item, this is a renewal of an existing declaration rather than a new project announcement, and the source text is unclear on the exact kilometer markers affected — so treat those figures as approximate.

*Source: Decree No. 2.26.573 of August 3, 2026.*

## 🎓 University leadership jobs get merit-based, competitive rules

New implementing decrees under last year's higher-education law (Law 59.24) replace older, more discretionary appointment methods for senior university jobs with defined, competitive procedures:

- University **department head** elections: secret ballot among tenured professors, in person or via a dedicated online platform, for a single non-renewable four-year term.
- University **financial-administrative director**: candidates must be Moroccan nationals with at least a master's degree and either prior department-head-level responsibility or 10 years of relevant experience (public or private sector).
- University **president and institution head** selection: open applications advertised on a public employment portal, screened and ranked by a dedicated committee that must include at least two women and an economics/finance specialist, narrowing to a shortlist of up to seven candidates who are then interviewed.

These are procedural/governance reforms rather than personnel announcements — they set the rules future leadership races will follow, rather than naming anyone to a post.

*Source: Decrees No. 2.26.328, 2.26.330, 2.26.502, 2.26.327 and 2.26.329, dated between July 23 and August 3, 2026.*
