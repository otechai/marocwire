## 💰 Morocco Locks In €423.8 Million World Bank Loan for Jobs and "Green Growth"

The Moroccan government formally approved a loan agreement worth **423,800,000 euros** (roughly $460 million) from the World Bank, signed on 6 May 2026. It's described as the "first development policy loan for employment and green growth" — World Bank language for a loan that isn't tied to a single infrastructure project, but is instead released in exchange for Morocco adopting specific policy reforms tied to job creation and environmental goals. The decree was signed by Head of Government Aziz Akhannouch and countersigned by the budget minister, Fouzi Lekjaa.

The gazette doesn't spell out the specific reform conditions attached to the loan, but the timing lines up closely with a separate government report (below) documenting deep, long-standing failures in how Morocco trains and re-skills its workforce — suggesting the loan money may be aimed at fixing exactly those gaps.

*Source: Décret n° 2-26-474, 2 July 2026*

## 📊 Morocco's Own Advisory Council Says the Job-Training System Is Failing Almost Everyone

A detailed report from the Economic, Social and Environmental Council (CESE), Morocco's official public-policy advisory body, delivers a blunt verdict on the country's job-training system: it barely reaches anyone who needs it, and the businesses that do benefit are overwhelmingly large corporations, not the small businesses that make up most of the economy.

The numbers are stark. Of roughly **315,000 companies** that pay Morocco's mandatory job-training tax, only **1,647** — less than **0.5%** — actually used the main training-funding program in 2022, the best year on record. Very small and medium businesses (TPME), the report's supposed priority target, received only about **622 million dirhams** (~$62M) in reimbursements over an entire decade, despite the tax pool collecting roughly **2.7 billion dirhams a year** ($270M+). Large companies, by contrast, captured nearly 25% of all trained employees despite representing a small share of firms. Refund waits average close to **nine months**, deterring small firms without cash reserves from even applying.

The report also flags that self-employed workers, informal-sector workers, and people who've lost their jobs are largely locked out of the system entirely, because eligibility is tied to formal payroll contributions (CNSS). A 2018 law meant to fix this (Law 60-17) has still not been implemented seven years later. Separately, a program to formally certify skills gained through work experience rather than diplomas — potentially significant for the many Moroccans, at home and abroad, who never finished formal schooling — has certified only **1,488 people since 2008**, even though nearly half of Morocco's working population holds no diploma at all. The Council is recommending an independent national training authority, a guaranteed 30% of training-tax revenue actually spent on training, and full-cost coverage for the smallest businesses.

*Source: CESE opinion, adopted 25 March 2026, published in this issue*

## 🏦 Popular Bank Cleared to Trade Financial Futures

Morocco's finance ministry approved **Banque Centrale Populaire** as an authorized "negotiator-clearer" on the country's financial futures market — essentially a license letting the bank trade and settle financial derivative contracts on behalf of clients. It's a technical but notable step in deepening Morocco's capital markets, potentially giving businesses and investors more tools to hedge currency, interest-rate, or commodity risk.

*Source: Arrêté n° 1280-26, 18 June 2026*

## 🛢️ Offshore Gas Exploration Deal Extended Off Morocco's Coast

The energy and finance ministries jointly approved a third amendment to the "RISSANA OFFSHORE" petroleum exploration agreement between Morocco's national hydrocarbons office (ONHYM) and **Chariot Rissana Limited** and **Chariot Offshore Gas Limited** (formerly Energean Morocco). The amendment extends the initial 12-month exploration period, trims a follow-on 12-month period, and adjusts the minimum work program for exploration permits covering "RISSANA OFFSHORE 1 through 5." No production or discovery figures are disclosed in this filing — it's a technical extension of exploration rights, not a discovery announcement.

*Source: Arrêté conjoint n° 1244-26, 12 June 2026*

## 🩺 New Foreign Medical Degrees Recognized as Equivalent to Moroccan Qualifications

Morocco's higher education ministry expanded its list of foreign medical diplomas recognized as equivalent to Moroccan degrees — relevant to any Moroccan (including diaspora members) who trained abroad and wants to practice at home. New additions include ophthalmology and cardiology specialty certificates from universities in Ukraine, general medical degrees from universities in Ukraine and Romania, and a medical degree from the University of Djibouti. Most of these require the holder to also complete supervised training in Morocco (two to three years) and pass an evaluation exam before the equivalence is confirmed.

*Source: Arrêtés n° 1199-26 through 1203-26, 11 June 2026*
