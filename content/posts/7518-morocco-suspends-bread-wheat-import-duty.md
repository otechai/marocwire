## 🌾 Government suspends the import duty on bread wheat to protect prices

Morocco's government has moved to keep bread affordable by suspending the import duty on common (bread) wheat and its derivatives. A decree first reinstated the duty for a two-month window, from June 1 to July 31, 2026, then — in the same text — ordered it suspended again starting August 1, 2026, overriding the standard tariff schedule that would otherwise apply. Bread wheat is Morocco's most politically sensitive food staple, and the government has repeatedly used import-duty suspensions like this one as a lever to shield consumers from global grain price swings without resorting to broader subsidies.

For Moroccans abroad who send money home or worry about the cost of living for family in Morocco, this is a concrete signal that the government is actively managing bread affordability going into the second half of 2026, rather than letting international wheat prices pass straight through to the loaf on the table.

*Source: Decree 2.26.419, dated 8 Hija 1447 (25 May 2026).*

## 💧 Morocco locks in roughly €193 million for a national water-stress project

Two decrees approve financing agreements between Morocco and the Islamic Development Bank worth a combined **$5.94 million and €182.6 million** (roughly €193 million all together) for a project titled "Mitigating the effects of water stress in the Kingdom of Morocco." The funding package includes a loan agreement plus a separate installment-sale financing and mandate agreement, both signed on 9 March 2026.

Morocco has faced years of severe drought, and financing on this scale — from one of the country's largest development-finance partners — signals continued major investment in water security infrastructure, relevant to anyone with family, farmland, or property depending on Morocco's water supply.

*Source: Decrees 2.26.399 and 2.26.400, both dated 18 Hija 1447 (4 June 2026).*

## 🩺 Morocco recognizes a batch of foreign medical and architecture degrees

The Ministry of Higher Education issued 13 orders updating the official lists of foreign diplomas recognized as equivalent to Moroccan medical and architecture qualifications — a process that directly affects Moroccans who studied medicine or architecture abroad and want their credentials recognized to practice back home. The newly recognized degrees span general medicine, nephrology, dermatology, ophthalmology, pathological anatomy, cardiovascular surgery, gynecology-obstetrics, and cardiology, plus one architecture qualification.

Countries covered in this batch include **Ukraine** (multiple medical specialties, from universities including Zaporizhzhia State Medical University, Bogomolets National Medical University, and Odesa National Medical University), **Senegal** (via Cheikh Anta Diop University, for pathological anatomy and traumatology-orthopedics), **Romania** (a general medicine degree), **Germany** (gynecology-obstetrics via the Lower Saxony medical board, and cardiology via the Westphalia-Lippe medical board), and a combined **Hungary/Ukraine** qualification recognized as equivalent to a Moroccan architecture degree.

*Source: Ministerial orders 899-26 through 911-26 and 989-26, all dated 16 Kaada 1447 or 2 Hija 1447 (4–19 May 2026).*
