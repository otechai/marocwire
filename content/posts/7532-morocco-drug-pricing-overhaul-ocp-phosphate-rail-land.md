## 💊 Morocco Rewrites the Rules for Drug Prices

The biggest story in this issue is a full overhaul of how Morocco sets prices for both locally-manufactured and imported medicines — a reform that could reshape household healthcare costs for millions. Under the new rules, imported drugs get a fixed **2.5% surcharge** added to their factory price to cover shipping and customs, and that price is converted to dirhams using an average of the central bank's exchange rate over the prior three months rather than a single snapshot rate.

The pricing formula for medicines is now split by cost tier: for **original branded drugs priced above 300 dirhams**, Morocco will match its price to the **lowest price found among a set of reference countries**; for cheaper originals (300 dirhams or under), it will use the **average price** across those countries. Generic drug prices are pegged to the same discount percentage applied to the branded original, capped at a maximum 15% further cut, and generics can never be priced higher than their branded equivalent. Prices for original drugs get re-reviewed **every three years**, and to avoid pointless bureaucratic churn, a price won't be revised at all if the change amounts to less than **1 dirham** (for drugs priced at 15 dirhams or under) or less than **2 dirhams** (for drugs between 16 and 70 dirhams).

The reform also gives health authorities new emergency powers: they can now negotiate special discount agreements directly with pharmaceutical manufacturers for very high-cost drugs, and — more importantly for patients — they can authorize selling a drug outside the normal pricing rules altogether during a health emergency, a severe shortage, or when a manufacturer can't meet national demand. Every currently marketed drug's price will be reviewed under these new rules within **12 months**, with the updated prices taking effect **60 days** after they're published.

*Source: Decree 2.25.631 amending Decree 2.13.852, dated July 23, 2026.*

## 🚄 More Land Cleared for the Kenitra–Marrakech High-Speed Rail Line

Morocco's Transport Ministry has updated the list of land parcels being taken over to build the high-speed rail line extending south from Kenitra to Marrakech — part of the broader push to extend the country's high-speed rail network beyond the existing Tangier–Kenitra–Casablanca "Al Boraq" line. The newly listed parcels sit in Jbilate commune, Rehamna province, in the Marrakech-Safi region, covering several kilometer-marker stretches of the planned route. This order amends and expands a land-relinquishment decision first made in May 2025, suggesting the project's land-acquisition phase is actively progressing.

*Source: Order 1289.26 amending Order 1350.25, dated June 19, 2026.*

## 🪨 OCP Launches a New Phosphate-Transport Corridor in Safi

Morocco has declared a new phosphate transport project in Safi province a matter of "public utility" — the legal trigger that allows the state to seize private land for it — clearing the way for **OCP Group** (the Chérifien Phosphate Office, Morocco's phosphate mining and fertilizer giant) to build a new corridor and associated facilities for moving phosphate and its derivatives out of the Chahda commune area. The right to actually carry out the land expropriation has been delegated directly to OCP's Director General, signaling the company itself — not just the government — is driving this expansion.

*Source: Decree 2.26.518, dated July 23, 2026.*

## 💧 Land Seized to Pipe Drinking Water to Drought-Hit Taounate

Two separate decrees clear land in Taounate province — a region that has repeatedly struggled with water access — to build new drinking-water infrastructure drawing from the **Idriss I dam** and the **Bouhouda dam**. One project will strengthen water supply to Tissa commune and its surrounding villages; the other will connect Ain Mediouna, Ain Maatouf, and Oulad Daoud communes to the same dam network. Both are run by Morocco's national water and electricity utility (ONEE), which has been given the authority to carry out the expropriations directly.

*Source: Decrees 2.26.539 and 2.26.540, both dated July 23, 2026.*

## 🎫 Morocco Launches a "Youth Pass" — Discounted Services for Ages 16–30

A new government-run digital pass, called **"Jawaz Chabab"** (Youth Pass), lets young people access reduced-price or free services across eleven sectors: culture and arts, youth programs, sports, health, education, vocational training, transport, employment, tourism, digital technology, and housing. The pass is available to young Moroccans and to foreign nationals legally residing in Morocco, aged 16 to 30, via a dedicated smartphone app. A coordination committee spanning 15 government sectors — including the ministry handling Moroccans-abroad affairs — will meet every six months to expand the list of participating benefits, and each year's youth ministry budget will carry a dedicated funding line to pay for it. Misuse of the pass (like letting someone else use your account) can get benefits suspended.

*Source: Decree 2.25.153, dated July 23, 2026.*

## 🏞️ Nearly 2,400 Hectares of Tribal Land Formally Mapped in Chichaoua and Essaouira

Six separate decrees formally confirm the official boundaries of collective ("sulaliyate" / tribal-community-owned) land parcels across Chichaoua and Essaouira provinces — a legal process that protects these lands from boundary disputes and formally registers ownership in the name of the local collective communities rather than any individual. Combined, the six parcels — named Tikidar, Bouznir, Melk Oulad Aissa, Chih 1, Draa Skoum, and Hrika — cover **roughly 2,397 hectares**. This kind of formal land-boundary determination is a recurring, sensitive issue in rural Morocco, where collective land rights (and disputes over them, including gender-equality fights over access) directly affect farming families' livelihoods.

*Source: Decrees 2.26.451, 2.26.521, 2.26.522, 2.26.523, 2.26.524, and 2.26.525, all dated July 23, 2026.*

## 🏭 New Rail Spur to Connect a Factory Straight to the National Network

A roughly 24-hectare plot in Sidi Yahya commune, Sidi Slimane province, is being transferred from state-owned "private" land into the formally protected "public domain" specifically to build a rail connection linking the **Koshin Hi-Tech** factory to Morocco's national railway network — a concrete, if narrow, signal of industrial investment getting direct freight-rail access.

*Source: Decree 2.26.475, dated July 23, 2026.*

## 👮 500+ Gendarmes Just Got Expanded Police-Investigator Powers

A joint order from the Head of Government and the Prosecutor General grants **"judicial police officer" status** to more than 500 members of the Royal Gendarmerie, listed by name and rank (mostly non-commissioned officers). This status expands their legal authority under Morocco's Code of Criminal Procedure to independently conduct investigations, take statements, and build case files — a meaningful expansion of frontline law-enforcement powers, even though it's formally an administrative appointment order.

*Source: Joint Order 1435.26, dated July 9, 2026.*

## ☢️ New Rules Open the Door to Mining Uranium and Thorium From Phosphate Waste

A more technical but strategically interesting update: Morocco has rewritten its licensing rules for extracting and processing minerals containing uranium or thorium — including specifically from the leftover byproducts of mining operations. Given that Morocco holds the world's largest phosphate reserves, and phosphate rock is known to contain uranium, this new regulatory chapter (covering everything from exploration permits to mine-closure plans) lays administrative groundwork that could matter if Morocco or OCP ever moves to commercially recover uranium as a byproduct of phosphate production — a resource increasingly prized amid the global race for critical minerals.

*Source: Decree 2.26.11 amending Decree 2.20.131, dated July 23, 2026.*
